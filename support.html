FIND YOUR SUPPORT

You don't have to carry the weight alone. There is a place for you.

This page is about finding help in your city. Remember: HEALING is a journey!

The first step is the hardest

Searching for help can feel overwhelming. When you're struggling with PTSD or mental health, the world can feel too loud, and the systems for getting help can feel too clinical and cold.

Whether you are looking for a professional therapist, a support group of people who "get it," or just a safe place to start, the most important thing is that you are looking. That is a victory in itself.

Finding help in your city

Since everyone's location is different, here is the best way to find local support that actually works for you:

1. Start with your General Practitioner (GP)

In most countries, your family doctor is the gateway. They can provide the necessary referrals to psychologists or PTSD specialists who are covered by insurance or the state.

2. Use Local Directories

Search for "Youth Mental Health Services" or "Community Trauma Centers" in your city. Look for places that mention "Trauma-Informed Care"—this means they understand how PTSD works.

3. Peer Support & Subcultures

Sometimes the best support isn't in a clinic. Look for local art collectives, music communities, or youth centers. Finding people who share your identity and struggles can be a powerful part of healing.

Trusted Global Resources

If you aren't ready for a local doctor yet, or can't find one, these organizations provide world-class guidance and support:

Mind (UK/International)

An incredible resource for understanding PTSD and finding self-care strategies. Visit Mind.org.uk

World Health Organization (WHO)

For factual, global data and guidelines on managing mental health conditions. Visit WHO.int

Sundhed.dk (Denmark)

The primary source for health information and professional pathways in Denmark. Visit Sundhed.dk
